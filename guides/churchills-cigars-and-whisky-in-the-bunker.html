Churchill's Cigars and Whisky in the Bunker

The famous habits Churchill kept up underground.

Deep beneath the streets of London, a secret world of wartime strategy and leadership played out in the Churchill War Rooms, where Winston Churchill and his team directed British efforts during World War II. Despite the grave circumstances, Churchill maintained some of his famous habits, including his love of cigars and whisky, even in the underground bunker.

Introduction to Churchill's Habits

Churchill's affinity for fine cigars and whisky is well-documented, and these habits brought him comfort and solace during the tumultuous years of the war. The Churchill War Rooms, now a museum, offer a unique glimpse into the life and routines of the former Prime Minister, including his beloved cigars and whisky.

Churchill's Cigar Collection

Churchill was particularly fond of Cuban cigars, and his favourite brand was Romeo y Julieta. He would often smoke up to 10 cigars per day, a habit that was not without its challenges in the underground bunker. To accommodate his love of cigars, a special air ventilation system was installed to remove the smoke and prevent it from interfering with the sensitive equipment and maps used for strategic planning.

Whisky and Fine Spirits

Churchill's love of whisky was equally renowned, and he often enjoyed a glass or two while working in the War Rooms. His favourite whisky was Johnny Walker Red Label, which he would frequently drink with a splash of soda water. Other fine spirits, including brandy and champagne, were also available in the War Rooms, and were often used to entertain visiting dignitaries and guests.

  • Johnny Walker Red Label was Churchill's whisky of choice
  • Brandy and champagne were also available for special occasions
  • Churchill's drinking habits were often tempered by his work schedule and responsibilities

Life in the War Rooms

Despite the comforts of his cigars and whisky, life in the War Rooms was far from luxurious. The bunker was a cramped and busy space, filled with maps, charts, and other equipment used for strategic planning. Churchill and his team worked tirelessly, often for long hours, to direct British efforts during the war. A visit to the Churchill War Rooms offers a fascinating insight into this period of history, and can be usefully complemented by a guided Westminster WWII walking tour, which adds context to the experiences of those who lived and worked in the area during this time.

Preserving History

The Churchill War Rooms have been meticulously preserved to reflect the conditions and appearance of the bunker during the war years. Visitors can see the very rooms where Churchill and his team worked, including the Cabinet Room, the Map Room, and Churchill's own private quarters. The museum also features a range of exhibits and artefacts, including Churchill's famous siren suit, which he wore on several occasions during the war.
