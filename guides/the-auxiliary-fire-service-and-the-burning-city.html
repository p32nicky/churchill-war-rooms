The Auxiliary Fire Service and the Burning City

Fighting the fires the Blitz left behind.

The Blitz, a period of intense bombing of London by the German Air Force during World War II, left the city in ruins, with fires burning across the capital. The Auxiliary Fire Service (AFS) played a crucial role in fighting these fires, working tirelessly to bring them under control and prevent further destruction.

Formation and Role of the Auxiliary Fire Service

The AFS was formed in 1938, with the aim of providing additional firefighting support to the regular fire services in the event of a national emergency. During the Blitz, the AFS was instrumental in responding to fires caused by incendiary bombs, which were designed to start large-scale fires. The AFS worked closely with the London Fire Brigade to tackle these fires, often in extremely challenging conditions.

Challenges Faced by the AFS

The AFS faced numerous challenges as they worked to extinguish the fires burning across London. These included:

  • Lack of equipment and resources, particularly in the early days of the Blitz
  • Difficulty accessing areas due to rubble and debris blocking roads
  • The risk of further bombing raids, which made it hazardous for firefighters to respond to incidents
  • The physical and emotional toll of working long hours in extreme conditions

Tactics and Strategies

The AFS developed various tactics and strategies to combat the fires, including the use of water, foam, and other extinguishing agents. They also worked to create firebreaks, demolishing buildings to prevent fires from spreading. In addition, the AFS played a key role in implementing the "fire brigade scheme", which involved dividing the city into sectors and allocating resources accordingly.

Impact and Legacy

The AFS made a significant contribution to the war effort, helping to protect London and its inhabitants from the devastating effects of fire. Their bravery and selflessness have been recognised as an important part of the city's history, and their legacy continues to be celebrated today. For those interested in learning more about this period, a guided Westminster WWII walking tour is a great way to add context to the experiences of the AFS and the people of London during this time.

Preservation and Commemoration

Efforts have been made to preserve the history of the AFS and the Blitz, including the creation of museums and memorials. The Churchill War Rooms, for example, provide a unique insight into the workings of the British government during this period, and the role of the AFS in responding to the crisis. Visitors can explore the underground bunkers and learn about the bravery and resilience of those who worked to protect the city.
