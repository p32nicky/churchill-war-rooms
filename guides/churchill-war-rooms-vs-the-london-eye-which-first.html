Churchill War Rooms vs the London Eye: Which First?

Ordering a Westminster day around the War Rooms and the London Eye.

Planning a day out in Westminster can be a daunting task, especially when faced with two of London's most popular attractions: the Churchill War Rooms and the London Eye. Both offer a unique glimpse into the city's history and landscape, but which one to visit first? The answer depends on your interests, schedule, and personal preferences, so let's break down the options to help you make the most of your day.

Understanding the Attractions

The Churchill War Rooms are a network of underground bunkers that served as the British government's command centre during World War II. This historic site showcases the rooms and corridors where Winston Churchill and his team directed the war effort, complete with period furnishings and artefacts. In contrast, the London Eye is a giant Ferris wheel offering breathtaking views of the city from high above the Thames.

Location and Accessibility

Both attractions are located in the heart of Westminster, within walking distance of each other. The Churchill War Rooms are situated on King Charles Street, near the Houses of Parliament, while the London Eye is on the South Bank of the Thames, close to Westminster Bridge. Visitors can easily walk between the two, or take a short bus or taxi ride. Be sure to check the attractions' websites for up-to-date information on accessibility and mobility access.

Planning Your Day

To make the most of your day, consider the opening times and crowd patterns at each attraction. The Churchill War Rooms tend to be busiest in the late morning and early afternoon, while the London Eye is often crowded during peak summer months. You may want to visit the War Rooms first, then take a stroll along the South Bank to the London Eye, or vice versa. Some popular options to consider include:

  • visiting the Churchill War Rooms in the morning, followed by a walk to the London Eye for a late afternoon or evening ride
  • starting with a London Eye ride, then heading to the Churchill War Rooms to avoid the lunchtime crowds
  • breaking up the day with a walk in St James's Park or a visit to nearby Westminster Abbey

Adding Context with a Guided Tour

A guided Westminster WWII walking tour is a great way to add context to your visit, exploring the surrounding area and learning more about the history of the War Rooms and the impact of the war on the city. These tours often cover key landmarks and memorials, such as the Cenotaph and the Winston Churchill Statue, and can help you appreciate the significance of the Churchill War Rooms and their role in British history.

Practical Considerations

Before heading out, be sure to check the attractions' websites for the latest information on tickets, accessibility, and special exhibitions. You may also want to consider purchasing a combined ticket or using a London tourist pass to save time and money. Additionally, leave some time for security checks and bag searches at the Churchill War Rooms, and be prepared for potential queues at the London Eye, especially during peak season.
