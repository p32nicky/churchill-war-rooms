Changing of the Guard Near the Churchill War Rooms

Where and when to watch the Changing of the Guard nearby.

The Changing of the Guard is one of London’s most recognisable ceremonies, and the closest regular post to the Churchill War Rooms is on the forecourt of Buckingham Palace. A short walk or a quick tube ride from the museum will bring you into the heart of the spectacle, where the pomp of the Household Division meets the city’s historic streets.

Where to watch the guard

Primary viewing point

The official viewing area is the broad stone terrace directly in front of Buckingham Palace. From here you can see the incoming and outgoing guards, the mounted regiment and the band in full uniform.

Alternative spots

  • St. James’s Park – the park’s pathways give a clear line of sight to the palace façade, and you’ll be surrounded by greenery.
  • Victoria Memorial – standing behind the monument offers a slightly elevated perspective and fewer crowds.
  • Westminster Bridge – a riverside view across the Thames captures the ceremony against the backdrop of the London skyline.

Best times of year

Seasonal considerations

The ceremony runs year‑round, but the experience varies with the weather and the calendar. Summer days provide longer daylight and a higher likelihood of a full band, while winter performances are shorter and may be accompanied by a more subdued musical selection.

Special occasions

  • State visits – the guard may be accompanied by additional troops and a larger band.
  • Royal birthdays – expect a slightly extended programme and a more elaborate musical repertoire.
  • Public holidays – crowds tend to be larger, so arriving early is advisable.

Getting there from the Churchill War Rooms

Walking route

A pleasant 15‑minute stroll takes you from the War Rooms, across Whitehall, past the Cenotaph and along the Mall to the palace forecourt. The walk passes several historic sites, including the Churchill Museum’s own entrance on King Charles Street.

Public transport

For those who prefer to save time, the nearest Underground stations are Westminster (District, Circle, and Jubilee lines) and Green Park (Piccadilly, Jubilee, and Victoria lines). A short walk from either stop leads directly onto the Mall.

Adding context

Consider joining a guided Westminster WWII walking tour while you’re in the area; it weaves together the wartime stories of the War Rooms, the palace’s role during the Blitz, and the ceremonial traditions you’ll witness.

What to expect at the ceremony

Sequence of events

The ceremony typically begins with the arrival of the new guard, escorted by a drum major and the regimental band. The old guard receives a formal handover from the captain of the guard, followed by a precise marching routine that includes a slow‑time march, a quick‑time march and a final salute.

Uniforms and music

Guardians wear the iconic red tunics and bearskin hats of the Foot Guards, while the mounted regiment dons dark blue tunics and black helmets. The band performs a mixture of traditional marches, royal anthems and, on special days, contemporary pieces.

Tips for a comfortable viewing experience

  • Arrive at least 30 minutes before the scheduled time to secure a good spot, especially in peak season.
  • Dress for the weather; the ceremony takes place outdoors and can be chilly in winter.
  • Bring a small cushion or blanket if you plan to stand for an extended period.
  • Keep an eye on official notices for any changes due to royal engagements or security measures.
  • Respect the guard – photography is allowed, but avoid flash or any behaviour that could be seen as disruptive.
