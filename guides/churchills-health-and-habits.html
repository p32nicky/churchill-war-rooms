Churchill's Health and Habits

Naps, cigars, brandy and the routine that kept him going.

Winston Churchill's leadership during World War II is the stuff of legend, but behind the iconic statesman was a man with a unique set of habits and routines that helped him cope with the stresses of war. A visit to the Churchill War Rooms in London offers a fascinating glimpse into the daily life of the former Prime Minister, and understanding his health and habits can add a new layer of depth to your appreciation of this pivotal moment in history.

Introduction to Churchill's Daily Routine

Churchill was known for his rigorous daily routine, which began early in the morning and often lasted well into the night. This routine was carefully calibrated to maximise his productivity and help him cope with the demands of leading the country through its darkest hour. At the heart of this routine were a series of habits that might seem surprising to modern audiences, but which played a crucial role in maintaining his physical and mental health.

Naps and Sleep

One of the most famous aspects of Churchill's daily routine was his love of naps. He believed that a short sleep in the early afternoon could help to refresh his mind and body, and would often retire to his bedroom for a few hours of rest. This habit was not just a quirk – it was a deliberate strategy for managing the stresses of war leadership, and one that allowed him to stay focused and alert throughout the day. In fact, Churchill was known to be at his most productive in the late evening and early morning hours, when others might be flagging.

Cigars and Other Vices

Churchill was also famous for his love of cigars, which he smoked throughout his life. While this habit might seem alarming to modern health-conscious audiences, it was just one of several vices that Churchill enjoyed. He was also a heavy drinker, and was particularly fond of brandy – a habit that he shared with many of his contemporaries. Some of his favourite tipples included:

  • Pol Roger champagne
  • Scotch whisky
  • Fine brandy from France

Eating Habits and Exercise

Churchill's eating habits were also noteworthy, and reflected his love of rich, hearty food. He was particularly fond of roast beef, and would often eat large meals in the evening – a habit that was not always conducive to good health. Despite this, Churchill remained remarkably active throughout his life, and enjoyed a range of outdoor pursuits including painting, hunting, and walking. A guided Westminster WWII walking tour is a great way to add context to your visit to the Churchill War Rooms, and can help you understand the physical and geographical landscape that shaped Churchill's experiences during the war.

Maintaining Mental Health

Perhaps the most impressive aspect of Churchill's routine, however, was his ability to maintain his mental health during a period of extreme stress and uncertainty. This was no easy feat – the pressures of war leadership took a heavy toll on many of Churchill's contemporaries, and the Prime Minister himself was not immune to the psychological strain. And yet, through a combination of hard work, rest, and relaxation, Churchill was able to stay focused and motivated throughout the war, inspiring his countrymen and women to do the same.

Legacy of Churchill's Habits

Today, visitors to the Churchill War Rooms can gain a unique insight into the daily habits and routines that helped to shape Churchill's leadership during World War II. From the maps and charts that lined the walls of the War Cabinet room, to the humble bedrooms and bathrooms where Churchill rested and relaxed, every aspect of the War Rooms offers a fascinating glimpse into the life and times of this remarkable statesman. By understanding the health and habits that underpinned Churchill's success, we can gain a deeper appreciation for the man behind the legend – and the incredible achievements that he was able to accomplish during his time in office.
