Downing Street and Whitehall: A Walking Guide

Explore the heart of British government beside the War Rooms.

Located in the heart of London, the Churchill War Rooms are a must-visit attraction for anyone interested in British history, particularly the Second World War. However, there's more to explore in the surrounding area, which has been the centre of British government for centuries. This walking guide will take you through the historic streets of Downing Street and Whitehall, offering a glimpse into the country's rich past and its role in shaping the world.

Introduction to Whitehall

Whitehall is a street in the City of Westminster that has been the site of various government buildings for over 500 years. The street is named after the Palace of Whitehall, which was the main residence of the English monarchs from 1530 until it was destroyed by a fire in 1698. Today, Whitehall is home to numerous government departments and ministries, including the Ministry of Defence and the Cabinet Office.

Downing Street and its Famous Residents

Downing Street is one of the most famous streets in the world, and for good reason. It has been the official residence of British prime ministers since 1735, with the current prime minister living in 10 Downing Street. The street is also home to other important government buildings, including the Chancellor of the Exchequer's office at 11 Downing Street. Visitors can take a stroll down the street, but be aware that it is heavily guarded and not open to the public.

  • Take a photo in front of the iconic 10 Downing Street door
  • Visit the nearby St. James's Park for a peaceful stroll
  • Explore the nearby Horse Guards Parade, where the Trooping the Colour ceremony takes place

Historic Landmarks in Whitehall

Whitehall is home to many historic landmarks that showcase Britain's rich history. The Cenotaph, a memorial to those who died in both World Wars, is located in the centre of Whitehall and is the site of the annual Remembrance Sunday ceremony. The street is also lined with various statues and monuments, including the famous Mountbatten statue.

Adding Context with a Guided Tour

A great way to add context to your walking tour is by joining a guided Westminster WWII walking tour. This will provide you with a deeper understanding of the area's history and its role in the Second World War. The tour will take you through the streets of Whitehall and Downing Street, pointing out key landmarks and sharing stories about the people who shaped the war effort.

Other Points of Interest in the Area

In addition to the Churchill War Rooms, there are many other points of interest in the area that are worth visiting. The Banqueting House, designed by Inigo Jones, is the only surviving building of the Palace of Whitehall and features an impressive ceiling painted by Peter Paul Rubens. The nearby Big Ben and the Houses of Parliament are also must-visit attractions, offering a glimpse into the British political system.

  • Visit the Churchill War Rooms to learn about Winston Churchill's leadership during WWII
  • Take a tour of the Houses of Parliament to see the British political system in action
  • Explore the nearby Westminster Abbey, a beautiful Gothic church that has been the site of coronations, royal weddings, and state funerals for centuries
