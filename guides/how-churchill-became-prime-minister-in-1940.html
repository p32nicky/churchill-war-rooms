How Churchill Became Prime Minister in 1940

The political drama that put Churchill in Number 10.

Winston Churchill's ascent to the role of Prime Minister in 1940 was a pivotal moment in British history, marking a significant shift in the country's leadership during a time of great turmoil. The events that led to his appointment are a fascinating tale of political intrigue and circumstance.

Background to the Crisis

In the early months of 1940, Britain was already at war with Germany, and the government, led by Prime Minister Neville Chamberlain, was facing increasing criticism for its handling of the conflict. The British Expeditionary Force had been deployed to France, but the German army was making rapid advances, and the situation was becoming increasingly dire.

The Fall of Chamberlain

As the military situation in France deteriorated, Chamberlain's position became untenable. On 7 May 1940, the House of Commons began a debate on the British withdrawal from Norway, which had been a disaster. The debate turned into a vote of no confidence in Chamberlain's government, and although he survived the vote, it was clear that his authority had been severely damaged. Chamberlain resigned on 10 May, and the process of finding a new Prime Minister began.

Churchill's Rise to Power

Churchill, who had been serving as First Lord of the Admiralty, was the obvious candidate to succeed Chamberlain. He had been a vocal critic of the government's policy of appeasement towards Nazi Germany and had been a strong advocate for British rearmament. Churchill's experience and leadership qualities made him the ideal candidate to lead the country during a time of war.

  • His role in the Admiralty had given him a deep understanding of the military situation and the importance of naval power.
  • He had a strong reputation as a skilled orator and was able to inspire and rally the British people.
  • He had a deep understanding of the complexities of international politics and was well-respected by other world leaders.

Forming a Coalition Government

Churchill's first task as Prime Minister was to form a coalition government, bringing together members of the Conservative, Labour, and Liberal parties. This was a challenging task, as the different parties had significant ideological differences, but Churchill was able to use his charm and negotiating skills to bring them together. The resulting government was a strong and united team, dedicated to winning the war.

Adding Context to Churchill's Story

To fully understand the significance of Churchill's appointment as Prime Minister, it is helpful to consider the broader historical context. A guided Westminster WWII walking tour is a great way to add context to Churchill's story, providing a deeper understanding of the events and circumstances that led to his rise to power. By exploring the streets and landmarks of Westminster, visitors can gain a deeper appreciation for the history of the time and the role that Churchill played in shaping Britain's response to the war.
