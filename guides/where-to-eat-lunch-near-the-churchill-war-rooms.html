Where to Eat Lunch Near the Churchill War Rooms

Lunch spots around Westminster and St James's near the War Rooms.

Just a short walk from the Churchill War Rooms, the streets of Westminster and St James’s offer a surprisingly diverse lunchtime scene. Whether you’re craving a hearty British roast, a light salad with a view of the Thames, or a quick bite between museum visits, the neighbourhood provides options that suit every palate and timetable. Below is a guide to some of the most reliable spots, grouped by the type of experience they deliver.

Classic Pub Grub with a Historic Twist

Westminster’s pubs have been serving locals and travellers for centuries, and many have retained the cosy atmosphere that makes a post‑museum lunch feel like a step back in time. Look for establishments that keep their original wood paneling, low‑level lighting and a selection of real ales on tap.

  • The Red Lion – A stone’s throw from the War Rooms, this pub offers a traditional menu of fish‑and‑chips, shepherd’s pie and a well‑stocked bar. The outdoor seating area is ideal for sunny days.
  • The Westminster Arms – Situated near the Houses of Parliament, it serves a Sunday‑style roast all week and has a quiet upstairs lounge for a more relaxed bite.
  • The St James’s Tavern – Known for its generous portions of bangers and mash, this venue also provides a selection of vegetarian pies and a respectable wine list.

Elegant European Bistros

If you prefer a slightly more refined setting, the St James’s side of the river hosts several bistros that blend continental flavours with British hospitality. Reservations are advisable during peak lunch hours, especially on weekdays when office workers flood the area.

  • Le Petit Café – A French‑inspired eatery offering a daily set menu that includes a starter, main and coffee. Expect dishes such as quiche Lorraine, grilled sea bass and a classic crème brûlée.
  • Osteria Basilico – Specialising in simple Italian fare, this trattoria serves fresh pasta, panini and seasonal salads. Their house‑made focaccia pairs nicely with a glass of Prosecco.
  • Casa de Tapas – A Spanish‑style bar with a vibrant ambience, perfect for sharing small plates like patatas bravas, chorizo al vino and grilled prawns.

Health‑Focused Cafés and Light Bites

For those watching their calorie intake or simply after a lighter meal, the neighbourhood boasts several cafés that prioritise fresh, locally sourced ingredients. Many provide gluten‑free and vegan options without compromising on flavour.

  • Green Leaf Café – Offers a rotating selection of grain bowls, avocado toast and cold‑pressed juices. Their indoor garden creates a tranquil backdrop for a quick lunch.
  • Purely Organic Kitchen – Known for its wholesome soups, salads and protein‑rich wraps. The menu clearly labels allergens, making it easy to navigate dietary restrictions.
  • Tea & Tattle – A charming spot for a light lunch of artisan sandwiches, scones and an extensive tea selection. Their Earl Grey latte is a favourite among visitors.

Quick Street‑Food Options

When time is of the essence, the streets surrounding the War Rooms feature several stalls and take‑away outlets that serve quality food at a reasonable price. These are perfect for grabbing a bite before or after a guided Westminster WWII walking tour, allowing you to keep your schedule flexible.

  • Westminster Street Grill – Serves gourmet burgers, halloumi wraps and hand‑cut chips from a retro‑styled kiosk.
  • St James’s Market Food Court – Hosts a variety of vendors offering everything from falafel wraps to sushi rolls.
  • Piccadilly Picnic – A mobile cart specialising in artisan cheese boards, fresh fruit and cold sandwiches, ideal for a portable lunch by the river.

Fine‑Dining with a View

For a special treat, a handful of restaurants near the river provide a more upscale experience, complete with panoramic vistas of the Thames and the iconic skyline. While these venues are pricier, they are worth considering for a leisurely lunch after a thorough exploration of the War Rooms.

  • The Riverbank Room – Located on the first floor of a historic building, it offers a seasonal tasting menu that showcases British seafood and game, complemented by an extensive wine list.
  • St James’s Terrace – Features floor‑to‑ceiling windows overlooking St James’s Park. The menu blends classic British dishes with contemporary twists, such as beetroot‑cured salmon and lamb rump with rosemary jus.
  • Clarendon House Restaurant – Set within a Georgian townhouse, it provides an elegant setting for a multi‑course lunch, with options for both meat‑eaters and vegetarians.
