The Home Guard and Britain's Last-Ditch Defence

The volunteers who prepared to defend Britain.

During World War II, the threat of a German invasion loomed large over Britain, prompting the formation of the Home Guard, a citizen army of volunteers who prepared to defend their country against all odds. The Home Guard played a crucial role in Britain's last-ditch defence, and their story is an integral part of the country's wartime history.

Formation and Role of the Home Guard

The Home Guard was formed in May 1940, following a call to action by Secretary of State for War Anthony Eden, who appealed for men to come forward and defend their country. The response was overwhelming, with hundreds of thousands of volunteers stepping forward to join the ranks. The Home Guard's primary role was to act as a secondary defence force, providing support to the regular army in the event of an invasion. They were tasked with defending key locations such as bridges, roads, and communication hubs, as well as conducting patrols and gathering intelligence.

Training and Equipment

Home Guard volunteers underwent rigorous training, which included instruction in firearms, first aid, and combat tactics. They were also taught how to use makeshift weapons, such as Molotov cocktails and improvised explosives. In terms of equipment, the Home Guard was initially poorly equipped, with many volunteers having to provide their own uniforms and weapons. However, as the war progressed, the Home Guard became better equipped, with the government providing them with rifles, machine guns, and other military hardware. Some of the key equipment used by the Home Guard included:

  • Rifles and machine guns for combat and defence
  • Anti-tank guns and explosives for defending against armoured vehicles
  • Radio equipment for communication and intelligence gathering

Notable Home Guard Units

Some Home Guard units gained particular notoriety for their bravery and ingenuity. One such unit was the Auxiliary Units, a secretive group of Home Guard volunteers who were trained to conduct guerrilla warfare behind enemy lines in the event of an invasion. These units were equipped with specialized equipment, including explosives and sniper rifles, and were tasked with disrupting enemy supply lines and communication networks.

Legacy of the Home Guard

The Home Guard played a significant role in Britain's wartime history, and their legacy continues to be felt today. The Home Guard's bravery and dedication to their country inspired a nation, and their story has been immortalized in literature, film, and television. For those interested in learning more about the Home Guard and Britain's wartime history, a visit to the Churchill War Rooms is a must. A guided Westminster WWII walking tour is also a great way to add context to your visit, providing a deeper understanding of the events and people that shaped Britain's wartime experience.

Preserving the History of the Home Guard

Efforts have been made to preserve the history of the Home Guard, with many museums and archives dedicated to showcasing their story. The Churchill War Rooms, for example, feature exhibits on the Home Guard, including personal accounts and artefacts from the period. These exhibits provide a unique insight into the lives of the Home Guard volunteers, and serve as a reminder of the important role they played in Britain's wartime history. By preserving the history of the Home Guard, we can ensure that their bravery and sacrifice are never forgotten, and that their legacy continues to inspire future generations.
