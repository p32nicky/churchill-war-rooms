What Survived Untouched Since 1945

The objects frozen in time inside the War Rooms.

The Churchill War Rooms, hidden beneath the streets of London, are a fascinating piece of history that has remained largely untouched since the end of World War II. As you step inside, you are transported back to a time of great turmoil and uncertainty, surrounded by the very objects that played a crucial role in shaping the course of the war.

Introduction to the War Rooms

The War Rooms were the secret underground headquarters from which Winston Churchill led Britain to victory. The complex of rooms and corridors, built in the 1930s, was designed to be a secure and self-sufficient facility, equipped with all the necessities for the war effort. Today, the War Rooms are a museum, offering a unique glimpse into the past, with many of the original objects and fixtures still in place.

Maps and Charts

The War Rooms are filled with an incredible array of maps and charts, which were used to track the progress of the war. From large wall maps of Europe and Africa to smaller, more detailed charts of specific theatres of war, these visual aids played a vital role in planning and strategy. Many of the maps still have the original markings and annotations, made by Churchill and his team, giving a poignant insight into the decision-making process.

  • Coloured pins and flags were used to indicate the positions of Allied and enemy forces
  • Hand-drawn notes and comments can be seen on many of the maps, highlighting key events and battles
  • Some of the maps are still covered in a layer of dust, having been left undisturbed for decades

Communications and Technology

Communication was key to the war effort, and the War Rooms were equipped with the latest technology of the time. The complex had its own telephone exchange, teleprinters, and radio equipment, allowing Churchill and his team to stay in touch with other leaders and commanders around the world. Many of these original communication devices are still on display, giving a glimpse into the cutting-edge technology of the era.

Personal Quarters and Artifacts

As well as the formal meeting rooms and strategy areas, the War Rooms also include Churchill's personal quarters, which have been left largely untouched since 1945. The PM's bedroom, complete with his bed and dressing table, is a poignant reminder of the sacrifices made by those in power during times of war. Other personal artifacts, such as Churchill's favourite armchair and his iconic cigars, add a human touch to the museum.

A guided Westminster WWII walking tour is a great way to add context to your visit, exploring the surrounding area and learning more about the history of the war and its impact on the city.

Preservation and Conservation

Today, the War Rooms are a thriving museum, attracting visitors from all over the world. To preserve the historic integrity of the complex, the museum has undertaken a range of conservation and restoration projects, ensuring that the site remains as authentic as possible. From the original paintwork and fixtures to the historic artifacts and documents, every effort has been made to maintain the War Rooms in their original state, as a testament to the people and events that shaped the course of history.
