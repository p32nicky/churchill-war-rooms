Churchill War Rooms with a Baby or Pushchair

Practical advice for visiting the underground War Rooms with a buggy.

The Churchill War Rooms, a historic underground complex that served as Winston Churchill's command centre during World War II, is a must-visit attraction in London. While it may seem daunting to navigate the site with a baby or pushchair, with some planning and knowledge, you can have a successful and enjoyable visit with your little one.

Accessibility and Entrance

The Churchill War Rooms are located beneath the streets of Westminster, and the main entrance is on King Charles Street. The site is accessible for pushchairs, with a lift connecting the street level to the underground complex. However, it is recommended to contact the attraction in advance to confirm their current accessibility arrangements and to discuss any specific needs you may have.

Pushchair-Friendly Routes and Exhibits

Once inside, you will find that the War Rooms are relatively pushchair-friendly, with wide paths and gentle slopes. The majority of the exhibits are also accessible, including the Churchill Museum, the Cabinet War Rooms, and the interactive displays. However, some areas may be narrower or have uneven flooring, so it is essential to be mindful of these when navigating with a pushchair.

  • The Churchill Museum has a dedicated area for families, with interactive exhibits and activities suitable for young children.
  • The Cabinet War Rooms have been preserved to reflect their wartime appearance, with narrow corridors and small rooms, but are still accessible with a pushchair.

Facilities for Families with Babies

The Churchill War Rooms offer various facilities to support families with babies, including baby-changing facilities and a dedicated area for feeding. These facilities are located near the entrance and are clearly signposted. Additionally, the site has a café where you can take a break and enjoy some refreshments with your baby.

Planning Your Visit

To make the most of your visit with a baby or pushchair, it is crucial to plan ahead. Consider visiting during less busy periods, such as weekdays, to avoid large crowds. You may also want to take advantage of the audio guides, which provide a wealth of information about the history of the War Rooms and can be enjoyed at your own pace. For a more comprehensive understanding of the wartime period, a guided Westminster WWII walking tour is a great way to add context to your visit, exploring the surrounding area and its significant landmarks.

Tips and Essentials

When visiting the Churchill War Rooms with a baby or pushchair, there are a few essentials to keep in mind. Be sure to bring any necessary items, such as nappies, formula, and snacks, as well as a carrier or sling for your baby in case you need to navigate any areas that are not pushchair-friendly. It is also a good idea to check the attraction's website for any specific advice or guidelines for visiting with a pushchair.

  • Wear comfortable clothing and shoes, as you will be doing a significant amount of walking.
  • Consider bringing a small backpack or bag to carry your belongings, leaving your hands free to manage your pushchair.
