Getting There

How to Get to the Churchill War Rooms from Heathrow Terminal 5

The quickest routes to the Churchill War Rooms from Heathrow Terminal 5 by Tube, train, bus, or on foot.

Located in the heart of London, the Churchill War Rooms are a must-visit attraction for anyone interested in history, particularly the Second World War. If you're arriving at Heathrow Terminal 5, you'll want to know the quickest ways to get to this fascinating museum, and we've got you covered.

By Tube: The Fastest Route

The London Underground, or Tube, is the fastest way to get to the Churchill War Rooms from Heathrow Terminal 5. Take the Piccadilly line from the airport to Green Park Station, then transfer to the Jubilee line and get off at Westminster Station. From there, it's a short walk to the Churchill War Rooms. Be sure to check for any planned engineering works or line closures before you travel to avoid delays.

Train and Tube: A Convenient Alternative

Another option is to take the Heathrow Express train to Paddington Station, then take the Tube (Bakerloo line) to Westminster Station. This route may take a little longer than the direct Tube route, but it can be more comfortable, especially if you have a lot of luggage. You can also take a taxi from Paddington Station, but this will be more expensive than using public transport.

By Bus: A More Affordable Option

If you're on a budget or prefer to see more of the city, you can take the bus from Heathrow Terminal 5 to the Churchill War Rooms. The National Express coach service runs from the airport to Victoria Coach Station, from where you can take a short bus ride or taxi to the museum. This route takes longer than the Tube or train, but can be a more affordable option. Some of the bus routes you can take include:

  • National Express coach to Victoria Coach Station
  • Local bus services from Victoria to Westminster

On Foot: Not Recommended

While it's technically possible to walk from Heathrow Terminal 5 to the Churchill War Rooms, this is not a recommended route. The distance is around 17 miles, and the journey would take several hours, depending on your pace and how much luggage you have. Additionally, you'd need to navigate through busy streets and potentially heavy traffic, which can be stressful and tiring.

Adding Context with a Guided Tour

Once you arrive at the Churchill War Rooms, you may want to consider adding some context to your visit with a guided Westminster WWII walking tour. This can help you understand the significance of the museum and its location, as well as see other important historical sites in the area. The tour will take you to famous landmarks such as the Houses of Parliament, Big Ben, and Whitehall, and provide insight into the city's role during the war. Some popular sights you might see on the tour include:

  • The Cenotaph, a memorial to the war dead
  • The Ministry of Defence building, which played a key role in the war effort
  • Other important government buildings and historical landmarks

Planning Your Visit

Before you set off from Heathrow Terminal 5, be sure to check the Churchill War Rooms' website for up-to-date information on opening hours, ticket prices, and any special exhibitions or events that may be taking place during your visit. It's also a good idea to plan your route in advance, using a journey planner or map to help you navigate the city. With a little planning, you can have a smooth and enjoyable journey to this fascinating museum, and make the most of your time in London.
