The Churchill Statue in Parliament Square

The famous statue of Winston Churchill and where to find it.

The bronze statue of Sir Winston Churchill that dominates Parliament Square is a focal point for visitors to Westminster, offering a striking reminder of the man who steered Britain through its darkest hour. Whether you are tracing the steps of wartime leaders or simply pausing for a photo, the monument is easy to spot and worth a moment’s contemplation.

Location and surroundings

Parliament Square sits at the foot of the Palace of Westminster, framed by some of the capital’s most recognisable buildings. The Churchill statue occupies the centre of the square’s north‑east quadrant, directly opposite the iconic clock tower known as Big Ben.

The open layout allows unobstructed views from the surrounding streets, making the statue a natural meeting point for crowds and a convenient landmark for way‑finding.

History of the statue

Commission and design

The work was commissioned by the British government in the late 1960s as part of a broader effort to honour the nation’s wartime heroes. Renowned sculptor Ivor Roberts‑Jones was selected for his ability to capture both the gravitas and humanity of his subjects. He chose bronze for durability and for the dignified patina it develops over time.

Unveiling and public reaction

The statue was unveiled in 1970 by the Queen Mother, an event attended by senior politicians, members of the Royal Family and Churchill’s surviving relatives. Public reaction was largely positive, with many praising the likeness and the sense of resolve conveyed by the figure’s posture. Over the decades the monument has become a site for official commemorations, particularly on Victory in Europe Day and Churchill’s birthday.

Artistic details

Roberts‑Jones sculpted Churchill in his later years, wearing the familiar black suit, bow tie and the iconic “V” for victory sign. The statue stands on a granite plinth that bears an inscription reading “Winston Leonard Spencer‑Churchill, 1874‑1965”.

  • Pose: Right arm raised, left hand gripping the lapel of his coat – a gesture associated with his wartime speeches.
  • Facial expression: A determined gaze directed towards the Palace of Westminster, symbolising his resolve.
  • Material: Cast bronze, chosen for its resilience to London’s weather.
  • Plinth: Polished granite, providing a subtle contrast to the darker metal.

Visiting tips

The square is publicly accessible at all hours, but the best experience comes during daylight when the statue’s details are fully visible. Here are a few practical suggestions:

  • Arrive early on weekdays to avoid the midday tourist rush.
  • Use the nearby Westminster underground station (District, Circle and Jubilee lines) for the quickest access.
  • Wheelchair users will find smooth pathways around the square; the plinth’s steps are not required for viewing.
  • Photography is unrestricted, though flash may be discouraged during official ceremonies.
  • Combine a visit with a stroll to the nearby Westminster Abbey or the Houses of Parliament for a fuller historical context.

Contextualising Churchill in Westminster

Understanding why the statue occupies this prominent spot is easier when you consider the surrounding institutions. The square has long been a stage for political discourse, and Churchill’s presence links his wartime leadership to the ongoing work of Parliament. For those who wish to deepen that connection, a guided Westminster WWII walking tour offers insight into the decisions made within the nearby corridors, the role of the Cabinet, and how Churchill’s speeches resonated across the city. Such tours provide a narrative that ties the bronze figure to the very walls that once heard his famous “We shall fight on the beaches” address.
