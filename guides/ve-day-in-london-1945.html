VE Day in London, 1945

How London celebrated the end of the war in Europe.

On 8 May 1945, London erupted in joy as news of Germany's unconditional surrender spread, marking the end of the war in Europe, a momentous occasion that would come to be known as VE Day, or Victory in Europe Day. The city's streets were filled with jubilant crowds, and the atmosphere was electric, as people from all walks of life came together to celebrate this historic moment.

Background to VE Day

The road to VE Day was long and arduous, with the British people having endured years of bombing, rationing, and austerity. The war had taken a devastating toll on the city, with many buildings reduced to rubble and thousands of lives lost. However, as the Allies began to gain the upper hand, hopes of victory began to rise, and the news of Germany's surrender sparked an outpouring of emotion across the city.

Celebrations in London

As the news of the surrender broke, Londoners took to the streets, waving flags, cheering, and embracing one another. The city's iconic landmarks, such as Trafalgar Square, Piccadilly Circus, and Whitehall, were packed with people, all united in their joy and relief. The celebrations were spontaneous and infectious, with strangers becoming friends as they joined in the revelry.

  • Street parties were organised, with food, drink, and music adding to the festive atmosphere
  • Church bells rang out, adding to the cacophony of sound
  • Service personnel, who had been fighting abroad, were welcomed back as heroes

Key Locations in the Celebrations

Certain locations in London played a significant role in the VE Day celebrations, including Buckingham Palace, where the Royal Family appeared on the balcony to wave to the crowds. The Houses of Parliament, the Ministry of Defence, and the Churchill War Rooms were also focal points, as they had been the nerve centres of Britain's war effort.

A guided Westminster WWII walking tour is a great way to add context to these locations, allowing visitors to understand the significance of these sites and the role they played in the war.

Winston Churchill's Role in VE Day

Winston Churchill, the Prime Minister who had led Britain through the dark days of the war, played a significant role in the VE Day celebrations. He appeared on the balcony of the Ministry of Health, waving to the crowds and giving a victory speech, which was broadcast on the radio. His words captured the mood of the nation, as he declared, "The evil doctrines of Nazism have been beaten, and the Nazi regime, with all its crimes and cruelties, has been destroyed."

Legacy of VE Day

VE Day marked a significant turning point in British history, as the nation began to look to the future, rather than the past. The celebrations were a moment of catharsis, allowing the British people to release their emotions and come to terms with the trauma they had endured. Today, VE Day is still commemorated, with many Londoners gathering to remember the sacrifices made during the war and to celebrate the victory that was achieved.
