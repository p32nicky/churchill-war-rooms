Churchill War Rooms Directions: Finding the Entrance

How to find the Clive Steps entrance on King Charles Street.

The Churchill War Rooms lie hidden beneath the bustling streets of Westminster, and the most discreet point of entry is through the historic Clive Steps on King Charles Street. Whether you’re arriving by tube, bus or on foot, this guide will walk you straight to the doorway so you can descend into the wartime bunker without fuss.

Public transport routes to the vicinity

Underground

  • Westminster (District, Circle, Jubilee) – a two‑minute walk to King Charles Street.
  • St James’s Park (Circle, District) – exit onto Bridge Street and head east.
  • Waterloo (Bakerloo, Northern, Jubilee, Waterloo & City) – follow signs for the Houses of Parliament and turn onto Whitehall.

Bus services

  • Routes 11, 24, 87, and 148 stop at the Parliament Square/Whitehall stop.
  • Route 24 also serves the nearby Victoria station stop.
  • All these routes pause close to the Treasury building, a useful landmark for the entrance.

Walking from major stations

If you prefer a short stroll, exit Westminster station onto Bridge Street, turn left onto Whitehall, and continue until you reach the intersection with King Charles Street. The walk is under five minutes and offers a glimpse of the iconic Westminster façade.

Identifying King Charles Street

King Charles Street is a narrow, one‑way lane that runs east‑west between Whitehall and Parliament Square. It is often overlooked because it is flanked by imposing government buildings, but a few visual cues will keep you on track.

  • The street is marked by a series of historic lampposts and a modest brass plaque near the entrance.
  • Look for the Treasury building on the north side; the entrance sits opposite it.
  • Signage for “Clive Steps” is discreet but present, usually set into the stonework at street level.

From Whitehall, turn right onto King Charles Street; the walk is only a few dozen metres before you reach the steps.

Spotting the Clive Steps entrance

The Clive Steps are a set of stone stairs descending from street level into the underground complex. They are tucked between the Treasury and a modest office block, making them easy to miss if you’re not looking for them.

  • The steps are framed by a low stone balustrade and a simple bronze plaque reading “Clive Steps – Churchill War Rooms”.
  • A narrow doorway at the base of the steps leads to a ticket desk and a brief security screening.
  • Above the entrance, a small plaque commemorates Sir Robert Clive, after whom the steps are named.

When you reach the bottom, you’ll find a modern ticketing kiosk and a queue area. The descent takes only a few seconds, and you’ll emerge into the atmospheric underground corridor that leads to the museum.

Accessibility and nearby landmarks

The War Rooms strive to accommodate all visitors, though the historic nature of the entrance imposes certain limits.

  • A lift is available for wheelchair users; it is accessed from a side entrance on the same street, adjacent to the Treasury building.
  • Audio guides and tactile maps are provided on request at the ticket desk.
  • For those with limited mobility, the main museum entrance on Whitehall offers an alternative route with full lift access.

While you’re in the area, you can also explore the following landmarks, all within a short walk:

  • The Palace of Westminster and Big Ben.
  • St James’s Park, a green oasis ideal for a brief respite.
  • The Cenotaph on Whitehall, a solemn reminder of the First World War.

Practical tips for a smooth visit

Arriving prepared will make your descent into the War Rooms as seamless as possible. Keep these pointers in mind before you set off.

  • Check the museum’s website for any temporary closures or ticketing changes before you travel.
  • Allow extra time for security checks, especially during peak tourist periods.
  • Dress comfortably – the underground galleries can be cooler than the street above.
  • If you enjoy context, consider joining a guided Westminster WWII walking tour; it adds depth to the War Rooms experience without requiring a separate booking.
  • Take a moment to explore the small exhibition space on the surface level; it offers a preview of the underground displays.

With these directions and tips, you’ll find the Clive Steps entrance without hesitation and be ready to step back into the world of wartime strategy and leadership.
