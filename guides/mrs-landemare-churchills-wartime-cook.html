Mrs Landemare: Churchill's Wartime Cook

The cook who fed Churchill through the war years.

While Winston Churchill's leadership during World War II is the stuff of legend, the people behind the scenes who supported him are often overlooked. One such individual is Georgina Landemare, the cook who fed Churchill and his family throughout the war years, playing a vital role in keeping the Prime Minister's spirits and energy levels up during a tumultuous period in British history.

Who was Georgina Landemare?

Georgina Landemare, affectionately known as 'Mrs Landemare', was a skilled French-Swiss cook who worked for the Churchill family from 1940 until 1951. Born in 1883, she had previously worked for several aristocratic families in the UK and was renowned for her culinary expertise, particularly in French cuisine. Landemare's appointment as the Churchill's cook was a deliberate choice, as Winston's wife Clementine was keen to have a cook who could provide wholesome, nourishing meals that would sustain her husband throughout the war effort.

Cooking for the Churchills

Mrs Landemare's daily routine involved rising early to plan and prepare meals for the Churchill family, including Winston, Clementine, and their daughters. Her menus typically featured a range of dishes, from traditional British fare like roast beef and fish pie, to more exotic French-inspired creations. Landemare was also tasked with catering for the numerous guests who visited Chartwell, the Churchill's country estate in Kent, and 10 Downing Street, the Prime Minister's official residence in London.

Wartime Cuisine

During World War II, food rationing was in place, and Mrs Landemare had to be creative with the ingredients available to her. She made use of the Churchills' own kitchen garden at Chartwell, where fruits, vegetables, and herbs were grown, to supplement the family's diet. Some of the dishes she prepared included:

  • Soup made with vegetables from the garden
  • Meat pies using rationed meat
  • Preserved fruit from the estate's orchard
Despite the challenges posed by rationing, Landemare's culinary skills ensured that the Churchill family and their guests were always well-fed and entertained.

Life in the War Rooms

When Winston Churchill was in London, Mrs Landemare would often cook for him in the basement kitchen of the Churchill War Rooms, a secret bunker beneath the streets of Whitehall. The War Rooms, now a museum, provide a fascinating glimpse into the workings of the British government during World War II. For those interested in learning more about this period in history, a guided Westminster WWII walking tour is a great way to add context to a visit to the War Rooms, exploring the streets and landmarks that played a significant role in the war effort.

Legacy of a Wartime Cook

Georgina Landemare's contribution to the Churchill family's well-being during World War II should not be underestimated. Her dedication to her work and her ability to provide nourishing meals, even in the face of adversity, helped to sustain Winston Churchill throughout a period of immense pressure and responsibility. Today, the story of Mrs Landemare serves as a reminder of the often-overlooked individuals who worked behind the scenes to support those in power during times of war and crisis.
