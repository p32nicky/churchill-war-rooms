Churchill War Rooms Cloakroom and Luggage Storage

Where to leave coats, bags and luggage when you visit the Churchill War Rooms.

Visiting the Churchill War Rooms is a highlight of any London itinerary, but the underground nature of the attraction means you’ll need a sensible place to stash coats, bags and larger pieces of luggage. The on‑site cloakroom offers a secure, staffed solution, while nearby alternatives can handle bigger items or provide a quick drop‑off before you head into the historic underground complex.

On‑site cloakroom basics

The War Rooms operate a staffed cloakroom directly opposite the main entrance. It is open for the same periods as the attraction, though you should always confirm the latest times at checkout. The service is designed for short‑term storage of personal items while you explore the museum.

What you can store

  • Coats, jackets and umbrellas – ideal for the unpredictable British weather.
  • Backpacks, handbags and small suitcases – perfect for day‑trippers who have travelled light.
  • Personal electronics and valuables – staff will issue a ticket and keep items under supervision.

What you cannot store

  • Oversized luggage such as large suitcases or trunks.
  • Perishable food, liquids over a certain volume, or hazardous materials.
  • Items that require special handling (e.g., musical instruments, sporting equipment).

Fees and payment

The cloakroom charges a modest fee based on the size of the item and the length of storage. Rates are generally tiered – a small item for a few hours costs less than a larger bag kept for the whole day. Payment can be made by cash or card, and you’ll receive a numbered ticket to retrieve your belongings. Because pricing can change, always check the latest information on the official website or at the ticket desk.

Practical tips for a smooth visit

  • Arrive early. The cloakroom can become busy during peak times, especially on school holidays and weekends.
  • Keep your ticket safe. It is the only proof of ownership; losing it may mean you cannot reclaim your items.
  • Label your bags. A discreet label with your name and contact details speeds up the retrieval process.
  • Plan for larger luggage. If you’re travelling with a big suitcase, use a nearby train station locker or a dedicated luggage‑storage service.
  • Check the weather. A waterproof coat or a compact umbrella can be stored quickly, leaving you free to move about the underground corridors.

Nearby alternatives for larger or longer‑term storage

If the on‑site cloakroom does not meet your needs, several reputable options are within walking distance.

  • Railway station lockers. Both Westminster and St James’s Park stations have automated lockers available 24 hours a day. Sizes range from small cubbies to large units that can accommodate suitcases.
  • Dedicated luggage‑storage businesses. Companies such as Stasher or LuggageHero operate multiple venues around the Westminster area. You can book online, drop off your items, and collect them later in the day or the next morning.
  • Hotel concierge services. If you’re staying nearby, many hotels will hold luggage for a few hours after checkout, sometimes free of charge.

Combining your visit with a broader WWII experience

After you’ve explored the War Rooms, a guided Westminster WWII walking tour can put the underground headquarters into a wider historical context, linking the site to nearby landmarks such as the Cabinet War Rooms’ surface entrance, the Churchill statue and the wartime remnants of Whitehall. Joining a tour is a convenient way to deepen your understanding without needing to organise additional transport.
