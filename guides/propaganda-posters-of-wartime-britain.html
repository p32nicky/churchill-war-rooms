Propaganda Posters of Wartime Britain

'Keep Calm', 'Dig for Victory' and the war of words.

During World War II, the British government recognised the power of propaganda in shaping public opinion and boosting morale. Posters, in particular, played a significant role in conveying vital messages and promoting patriotic values. Many of these iconic posters have become synonymous with the wartime era, continuing to inspire and fascinate audiences today.

Introduction to Wartime Propaganda

Propaganda posters were a crucial tool in the war effort, used to inform, educate, and motivate the British public. The Ministry of Information, established in 1939, was responsible for producing and distributing these posters, which covered a wide range of topics, from recruitment and rationing to evacuation and air raid precautions. By using simple, yet powerful imagery and slogans, the government aimed to create a sense of national unity and purpose.

Iconic Posters of Wartime Britain

Some of the most iconic posters of the time have become ingrained in popular culture. The 'Keep Calm and Carry On' poster, designed in 1939, has become a symbol of British resilience, while the 'Dig for Victory' campaign encouraged people to grow their own fruits and vegetables, helping to alleviate food shortages. Other notable posters include those promoting recruitment to the armed forces, such as the 'Join the ATS' and 'Enlist Now' campaigns.

  • 'Keep Calm and Carry On' (1939)
  • 'Dig for Victory' (1941)
  • 'Join the ATS' (1941)
  • 'Enlist Now' (1940)

The War of Words

Propaganda posters were not just used to promote patriotic values, but also to demoralise the enemy and undermine their ideology. The British government used posters to highlight the atrocities committed by the Axis powers, as well as to promote the values of democracy and freedom. This 'war of words' was a crucial aspect of the propaganda campaign, aiming to sway public opinion and create a sense of moral superiority.

Design and Distribution

The design and distribution of propaganda posters were carefully planned and executed. The Ministry of Information worked with renowned artists and designers, such as Abram Games and Tom Purvis, to create visually striking and effective posters. These posters were then distributed in public spaces, such as train stations, schools, and community centres, to reach the widest possible audience. To add context to the role of propaganda in wartime Britain, a guided Westminster WWII walking tour is a great way to explore the streets and landmarks that played a significant part in the war effort.

Legacy of Wartime Propaganda

The propaganda posters of wartime Britain have left a lasting legacy, influencing the development of graphic design and advertising. Many of these posters have been preserved and are now held in museums and archives, serving as a testament to the power of visual propaganda. By examining these posters, we can gain a deeper understanding of the social, cultural, and historical context in which they were created, and appreciate the significant role they played in shaping public opinion during a time of war.
