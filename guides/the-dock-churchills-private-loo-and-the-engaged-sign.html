The Dock: Churchill's Private Loo and the 'Engaged' Sign

The in-joke behind the Transatlantic Telephone Room's lock.

The Churchill War Rooms, a labyrinthine complex of underground bunkers and rooms, served as the secret headquarters of the British government during World War II. One of the most fascinating aspects of this historic site is the myriad of stories and anecdotes that surround its various rooms and facilities, including the infamous 'Engaged' sign on the Transatlantic Telephone Room's door.

Introduction to the Transatlantic Telephone Room

The Transatlantic Telephone Room, also known as the 'Dock', was a secure communications facility that enabled Winston Churchill to converse with the President of the United States, Franklin D. Roosevelt, and other high-ranking Allied officials. This room played a crucial role in coordinating the war effort and allowed Churchill to maintain close relationships with his counterparts across the Atlantic.

The 'Engaged' Sign: An In-Joke

The 'Engaged' sign on the Transatlantic Telephone Room's door was not just a standard indicator of occupancy; it was also the subject of a clever in-joke among Churchill's staff. When the sign was displayed, it signified that Churchill was on a call with a high-ranking official, but it also served as a humorous way to deter unwanted visitors from disturbing the Prime Minister during his private conversations.

Churchill's Private Loo

Interestingly, the Transatlantic Telephone Room was also situated near Churchill's private lavatory, which was a rare luxury in the cramped and utilitarian War Rooms. This proximity has led some to speculate that the 'Engaged' sign may have been used to maintain the illusion that the room was always occupied, even when Churchill was simply using the facilities. While this theory is unconfirmed, it adds to the rich tapestry of stories surrounding the War Rooms.

  • The private lavatory was a rare comfort in the austere War Rooms
  • Its proximity to the Transatlantic Telephone Room has sparked speculation about the 'Engaged' sign
  • Churchill's staff often used humour to cope with the pressures of working in the War Rooms

Uncovering the Stories of the War Rooms

As you explore the Churchill War Rooms, keep an eye out for the various anecdotes and stories that are woven throughout the complex.
