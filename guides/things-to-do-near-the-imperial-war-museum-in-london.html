Things to Do Near the Imperial War Museum in London

Attractions, food and walks around the Imperial War Museum in Lambeth.

The Imperial War Museum in Lambeth sits on a vibrant stretch of South Bank, surrounded by a mix of historic sites, leafy riverside paths and a lively food scene. Whether you’re a history buff, a nature lover or simply looking for a bite after exploring the galleries, the neighbourhood offers plenty of options to fill a few hours.

Historic landmarks within walking distance

Just beyond the museum’s front doors you’ll find a cluster of sites that deepen the story of Britain’s wartime past. A short stroll takes you to the iconic Southbank Centre, whose modernist architecture contrasts with the older structures nearby, while the Royal Festival Hall often hosts exhibitions on the home front. Across the river, the HMS Belfast – a preserved World War II cruiser – offers an authentic glimpse of naval life. For a broader perspective, consider joining a guided Westminster WWII walking tour; it weaves together the area's military heritage with the political heart of the capital.

  • Southbank Centre – cultural hub with occasional wartime displays.
  • HMS Belfast – floating museum on the Thames, accessible by a short river walk.
  • Albert Embankment – lined with plaques commemorating local wartime contributions.

Riverside walks and parks

The Thames banks provide a tranquil counterpoint to the museum’s bustling interior. The Albert Embankment offers a flat, wheelchair‑friendly promenade ideal for a leisurely stroll, while the nearby Southwark Park rewards walkers with mature trees, ornamental gardens and a historic lake. If you prefer a more structured route, the Thames Path runs past the museum, linking you to the historic Southwark Cathedral and the bustling Borough Market area.

  • Albert Embankment – riverside promenade with views of the City of London.
  • Southwark Park – Victorian park with a café and children’s play area.
  • Thames Path – long-distance trail that passes through central London.

Food and drink options nearby

The vicinity of the Imperial War Museum is a culinary crossroads, offering everything from quick bites to sit‑down meals. On the South Bank, the Southbank Centre Food Market (operating on select days) serves street‑style fare ranging from Asian noodles to artisanal sandwiches. A few minutes’ walk inland, Vivat Bacchus on Lower Marsh offers a cosy wine bar with small plates, perfect for a post‑museum glass of red. For classic British comfort, head to The Anchor on the riverbank – a historic pub with a solid selection of ales and a menu of pies and fish‑and‑chips.

  • Southbank Centre Food Market – rotating stalls with international street food.
  • Vivat Bacchus – wine bar with sharing plates and a relaxed atmosphere.
  • The Anchor – traditional pub with riverside views and classic British dishes.

Cultural venues and galleries

Beyond the museum walls, the South Bank is a cultural corridor. The National Theatre presents a varied programme of contemporary and classic productions, while the Hayward Gallery within the Southbank Centre showcases cutting‑edge contemporary art. If you’re interested in design and architecture, the Design Museum in nearby Kensington (a short tube ride away) offers exhibitions that often touch on wartime innovation and its legacy.

  • National Theatre – world‑renowned productions, tickets available online.
  • Hayward Gallery – contemporary art exhibitions, often free entry.
  • Design Museum – design history with occasional focus on wartime technology.

Family‑friendly activities

Children will find plenty to keep them engaged after a visit to the Imperial War Museum. The museum’s own interactive displays are complemented by the nearby London Eye, which provides a spectacular aerial view of the capital – a thrilling experience for all ages. For a hands‑on adventure, the Sea Life London Aquarium offers marine exhibits that appeal to younger visitors. If the weather is fine, the open spaces of the South Bank promenade feature street performers and pop‑up art installations that add a playful element to the day.

  • London Eye – iconic Ferris wheel with panoramic city views.
  • Sea Life London Aquarium – marine life exhibits suitable for families.
  • South Bank promenade – open space with regular street entertainment.
