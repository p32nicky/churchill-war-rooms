Admiralty Arch: A Quick Guide Near the Churchill War Rooms

What to know about Admiralty Arch, a short walk from the Churchill War Rooms, and how to fit it into your day.

A short walk from the Churchill War Rooms, Admiralty Arch is a stunning landmark that offers a glimpse into London's rich history and architectural heritage, making it an excellent addition to your day out in Westminster.

Introduction to Admiralty Arch

Admiralty Arch is a grand building that was commissioned by King Edward VII in memory of his mother, Queen Victoria, and was designed by architect Sir Aston Webb, who also designed the façade of Buckingham Palace. The building was completed in 1912 and served as the official residence of the First Sea Lord, as well as the headquarters of the Admiralty, until the 1960s.

Architecture and Design

Admiralty Arch is a beautiful example of Edwardian architecture, with a mix of French and English influences. The building's design is dominated by a large arch, which spans the road and provides a dramatic entrance to The Mall, the grand avenue that leads to Buckingham Palace. The arch is flanked by two smaller arches, and the building's façade is adorned with intricate carvings and ornate details.

History and Significance

Admiralty Arch has played a significant role in British history, having served as the headquarters of the Admiralty during both World Wars. The building was also the site of many important meetings and events, including the planning of the D-Day landings during World War II. Today, Admiralty Arch is a Grade I listed building and is considered one of the most important and iconic landmarks in London.

  • It was used as a location for the 2012 Summer Olympics
  • It has been the subject of several restoration projects over the years
  • It has been sold to private developers for redevelopment into a luxury hotel and residential complex

Visiting Admiralty Arch

While Admiralty Arch is not open to the public for tours, it is still possible to admire its stunning architecture and learn about its history. A guided Westminster WWII walking tour is a great way to add context to your visit and learn more about the building's significance during the war, as well as its role in British history more broadly.

Practical Information

To plan your visit to Admiralty Arch, it's a good idea to check with your hotel or a local tourist information office for up-to-date information on the best ways to get there and what to expect when you arrive. You should also confirm opening times and any specific rules or restrictions that may be in place before you set out.
