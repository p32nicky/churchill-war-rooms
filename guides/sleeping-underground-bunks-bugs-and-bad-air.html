Sleeping Underground: Bunks, Bugs and Bad Air

The realities of living in the wartime bunker.

The Churchill War Rooms, hidden beneath the streets of London, served as the secret underground headquarters of the British government during World War II. For those who lived and worked here, life was far from comfortable, with cramped conditions, poor air quality, and limited amenities. This article explores the realities of living in the wartime bunker, from the bunks they slept in to the bugs that plagued them.

Living Quarters

The War Rooms were not designed as a residential facility, but as the war dragged on, staff found themselves spending more and more time underground. To accommodate them, makeshift living quarters were set up, with bunks and hammocks crammed into small rooms. The close proximity of colleagues and the lack of privacy made for a challenging living environment, both physically and mentally.

Health Concerns

The poor air quality in the War Rooms was a significant concern, with the smell of damp, mold, and cigarette smoke hanging heavy in the air. The ventilation system was inadequate, and the air was stale and musty. This led to a range of health problems, including respiratory issues and skin complaints. Additionally, the lack of natural light and exercise took its toll on the physical and mental wellbeing of staff.

Pests and Hygiene

With the War Rooms located deep underground, they were prone to infestations of pests, including rodents, cockroaches, and bed bugs. The close living quarters and poor hygiene facilities made it difficult to prevent the spread of diseases, and staff had to be constantly on the lookout for signs of infestation. To combat this, a range of measures were implemented, including:

  • Regular cleaning and disinfecting of surfaces
  • Use of insecticides and rodenticides
  • Installation of traps and screens to prevent pest entry

Daily Life

Despite the challenges, life in the War Rooms was not all doom and gloom. Staff worked hard to maintain a sense of normality, with routines and rituals helping to provide a sense of comfort and familiarity. From tea breaks to newspaper readings, staff found ways to make the most of their time underground. Legacy of the War Rooms

Today, the Churchill War Rooms are a testament to the ingenuity and determination of those who lived and worked here during World War II. The preserved bunker provides a unique glimpse into the realities of life underground, and serves as a reminder of the sacrifices made by those who worked tirelessly to support the war effort. As visitors make their way through the narrow corridors and cramped rooms, they can't help but feel a sense of respect for those who called this place home, if only for a time.
