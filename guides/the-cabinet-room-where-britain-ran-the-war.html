The Cabinet Room Where Britain Ran the War

How the War Cabinet met underground during the Blitz.

Deep beneath the streets of London, a secret network of rooms and corridors played a crucial role in Britain's war effort during World War II. At the heart of this underground complex was the Cabinet Room, where Winston Churchill and his government coordinated their response to the Nazi threat. Today, visitors can explore this historic site and gain a unique insight into the workings of the British government during the war.

Introduction to the War Cabinet

The War Cabinet was formed in 1939, with the primary objective of overseeing Britain's military strategy and response to the growing threat of Nazi Germany. The Cabinet was made up of senior government ministers, including the Prime Minister, the Chancellor of the Exchequer, and the Ministers of Defence and Foreign Affairs. As the Blitz intensified, the Cabinet began to meet in secret underground locations to ensure their safety and maintain continuity of government.

The Cabinet Room

The Cabinet Room was the central hub of the War Cabinet's operations, and it was here that many crucial decisions were made. The room itself is surprisingly small, with a large wooden table at its centre where the Cabinet members would gather to discuss and debate key issues. The room is still furnished with many of its original fixtures and fittings, including the iconic map table and the Prime Minister's chair.

Life Underground

As the Blitz raged on above ground, the War Cabinet and their staff worked tirelessly in the underground complex. The facilities were basic, but they included everything needed to support the government's operations, including a cipher room, a telephone exchange, and a series of sleeping quarters. The staff worked long hours, often in difficult conditions, but they were driven by a sense of duty and a determination to support the war effort.

  • Typical working days were long, often exceeding 12 hours
  • Staff had to adapt to the challenges of working in a underground environment, with limited natural light and ventilation
  • Despite the difficulties, morale remained high, with many staff members developing strong bonds and a sense of camaraderie

Key Decisions and Events

The War Cabinet made many significant decisions in the Cabinet Room, from coordinating military campaigns to managing the home front. Some of the key events and decisions made in the room include the response to the Dunkirk evacuation, the planning of the D-Day landings, and the development of Britain's atomic bomb programme. Visitors to the site can see many of the original documents and artefacts related to these events, including Churchill's own papers and correspondence.

Visiting the Churchill War Rooms

Today, the Churchill War Rooms are a popular tourist destination, offering a unique glimpse into Britain's wartime history. Visitors can explore the underground complex, including the Cabinet Room, and see many of the original fixtures and fittings. To add context to your visit, consider taking a guided Westminster WWII walking tour, which can provide a deeper understanding of the wartime history of the area and the significance of the War Cabinet's work. The site is a must-visit for anyone interested in history, politics, or the story of Britain's role in World War II.
